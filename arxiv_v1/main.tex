\begin{abstract}
A well-trained RL agent can be defeated by strategically perturbing its state observations at test time.
Sun \& Zheng (ICLR 2024) address this threat with \emph{belief-enriched pessimistic Q-learning}: the agent chooses a maximin action over a belief about possible true states, both when collecting data and when forming Bellman targets.
The resulting defense is strong in discrete domains but incurs substantial test-time cost, because every decision requires a maximin search over the belief.
We study a deliberately cheaper variant, \textbf{Pessimistic-Behavior / Greedy-Target} (PB-GT) Q-learning: training still uses pessimistic (maximin) \emph{behavior} under perturbed observations, but the \emph{target} is ordinary greedy Q-learning on the true next state, and test-time actions are greedy on the observed state---no belief, no maximin.
On a tabular $5{\times}5$ GridWorld with $500$ random seeds, full pessimistic Q-learning remains far more robust under trained DQN attackers ($-21.8{\pm}50.0$ vs.\ $-552.4{\pm}335.2$ for PB-GT and $-682.1{\pm}329.0$ for vanilla), while PB-GT matches vanilla inference speed and is ${\approx}11.6{\times}$ faster than maximin.
A Double-DQN port to CartPole-v1 ($5$ seeds) is reported as a preliminary deep-RL check: vanilla converges reliably after checkpoint restore, but our BP-style pessimistic and PB-GT agents do \emph{not} reproduce the GridWorld robustness ordering.
We therefore present PB-GT as a fast approximation with a documented robustness gap, not as a substitute for BP-DQN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reinforcement learning (RL) agents that act on learned value or policy networks are brittle under \emph{adversarial state perturbations}: a test-time attacker, subject to a budget $\varepsilon$, replaces the true state $s$ by a nearby observation $\tilde s\in B_\varepsilon(s)$ so as to minimize the agent's return~\citep{huang2017adversarial,zhang2020robust}.
The attacker does not change the underlying transition dynamics; it only poisons what the agent sees.
This threat model is now standard, and several defenses exist.
Regularization methods such as SA-MDP / SA-DQN~\citep{zhang2020robust} and WocaR-RL~\citep{liang2022wocar} encourage smoothness of $\pi(\cdot\mid s)$ across $B_\varepsilon(s)$.
Alternating-training methods such as ATLA~\citep{zhang2021robust} treat the attacker as an RL agent and co-train it with the defender, which can be more robust but is computationally expensive.

\citet{sun2024belief} take a different route.
They view robust RL under state attacks as a Stackelberg Markov game with asymmetric observations and propose \emph{pessimistic Q-learning}: given $\tilde s$, the agent selects
\begin{equation}
\pi(\tilde s)=\arg\max_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\min_{\bar s\in B_\varepsilon(\tilde s)} Q(\bar s,a).
\label{eq:maximin}
\end{equation}
The same maximin principle is used in the Bellman target, so training and testing are consistent.
They further shrink uncertainty with a belief $M_t\subseteq B_\varepsilon(\tilde s_t)$ updated from history (tabular case) or a particle-filter RNN (deep case: BP-DQN), and with diffusion purification for pixel observations (DP-DQN).
Empirically, BP-DQN and DP-DQN obtain strong robustness on continuous GridWorld and Atari.

Maximin-over-belief inference is the source of that robustness---and of its cost.
In our tabular GridWorld implementation, belief-restricted maximin is about $11{\times}$ slower per step than greedy $Q$-argmax; in a CartPole Double-DQN port the gap is about $50{\times}$.
Many settings need \emph{low-latency} decisions under moderate observation noise, not a full worst-case search at every tick.
This paper asks a narrow question that is complementary to~\citet{sun2024belief}:

\begin{quote}
\emph{If we keep pessimistic \textbf{behavior} while collecting data, but revert to vanilla greedy \textbf{targets} and greedy \textbf{test} actions, how much robustness do we retain, and how much inference cost do we save?}
\end{quote}

We call the resulting algorithm \textbf{PB-GT} (Pessimistic-Behavior / Greedy-Target) Q-learning.
It is \emph{not} ``off-policy DQN'': vanilla and pessimistic Q-learning are already off-policy in the classical sense.
The distinctive design is the \emph{pairing} of maximin behavior with a non-pessimistic backup and a non-pessimistic test policy---a deliberate train/test mismatch, opposite to the consistency argument of~\citet{sun2024belief}.
We evaluate it honestly, with the same three-way comparison throughout: vanilla Q / DQN, belief-enriched pessimistic Q / BP-style DQN, and PB-GT.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Tabular reproduction and scale.} We implement vanilla Q-learning and belief-enriched pessimistic Q-learning~\citep[Alg.~1--2]{sun2024belief} on a $5{\times}5$ GridWorld and run a $500$-seed comparison under fixed trained DQN attackers.
\item \textbf{PB-GT.} We define and evaluate a cheaper variant that uses pessimistic behavior at train time but greedy Double-Q targets and greedy test actions.
On GridWorld, PB-GT sits between vanilla and full pessimism under attack and matches vanilla inference speed.
\item \textbf{Preliminary deep port.} We port all three methods to CartPole-v1 with Double DQN and a simplified GRU particle belief (BP-DQN-\emph{style}, not full PF-RNN BP-DQN). Results are mixed; we do not claim GridWorld-style robustness in this setting.
\item \textbf{Scoped claim.} v1 documents a robustness--compute tradeoff. Closing the gap to BP-DQN while keeping PB-GT's speed, and comparing on continuous GridWorld / CAR-DQN / Atari, is left to future work.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

\subsection{MDPs and Q-learning}
An MDP is $\langle \mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,\gamma\rangle$.
A (deterministic) policy $\pi:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{A}$ induces $Q^\pi(s,a)=R(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'}P(s'|s,a)\max_{a'}Q^\pi(s',a')$ at optimality.
Vanilla Q-learning~\citep{watkins1992q} updates
\begin{equation}
Q(s,a)\leftarrow (1-\alpha)Q(s,a)+\alpha\bigl(R+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')\bigr)
\label{eq:vanilla-q}
\end{equation}
from true-state transitions, with $\varepsilon$-greedy behavior on $s$.
Deep Q-networks~\citep{mnih2015human} replace the table by $Q_\theta$, typically with Double DQN targets~\citep{van2016deep} and a replay buffer.
At test time vanilla DQN acts greedily on whatever observation it receives.

\subsection{State-adversarial attacks}
Following~\citet{huang2017adversarial,zhang2020robust,sun2024belief}, a test-time attacker observes $s_t$ and produces $\tilde s_t\in B_\varepsilon(s_t)$ (an $\ell_p$ ball; in our tabular grid, the discrete $1$-step neighborhood).
The agent acts on $\tilde s_t$; the environment transitions from the \emph{true} $s_t$.
For a fixed defender $\pi$, the attacker problem is itself an MDP~\citep{zhang2021robust,sun2024belief} with reward $-R$, so a DQN can be trained to attack a frozen defender.
We adopt the usual training assumption: the agent has access to an intact MDP and to $\varepsilon$ (or an estimate) while learning~\citep{zhang2020robust,sun2024belief}.

\subsection{Pessimistic Q-learning \citep{sun2024belief}}
Given $\tilde s$, Algorithm~1 of~\citet{sun2024belief} sets the target/behavior policy to the maximin rule~\eqref{eq:maximin} and sets the attacker's best response to
\begin{equation}
\omega_\pi(s)=\arg\min_{\tilde s\in B_\varepsilon(s)} Q\bigl(s,\pi(\tilde s)\bigr).
\label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
The Q update uses a pessimistic bootstrap $Q(s',\pi(\omega_\pi(s')))$.
Algorithm~2 maintains a belief $M_t$: $M_0=B_\varepsilon(\tilde s_0)$ (in our tabular code, $M_0=\mathcal{S}$), then
\begin{equation}
M_{t+1}=\{s':\exists s\in M_t,\,P(s'|s,a_t)>0\}\;\cap\; B_\varepsilon(\tilde s_{t+1}).
\label{eq:belief}
\end{equation}
Maximin is then taken over $M_t\cap B_\varepsilon(\tilde s_t)$ rather than the full ball.
BP-DQN (Alg.~4--5) lifts this idea to deep Q-learning with PF-RNN particles~\citep{ma2020pfrnn}; DP-DQN (Alg.~6--7) uses diffusion purification on pixels.
Our deep pessimistic agent is \textbf{BP-style}: maximin over a finite particle belief and a maximin TD target, but with a simplified GRU belief, not the full PF-RNN of~\citet{sun2024belief}.
We do not implement DP-DQN.

\label{sec:methods}

All three methods share the same environments, attacker protocol, and (in the deep case) the same vanilla backbone.

\subsection{Vanilla Q / Double DQN}
Tabular: $\varepsilon$-greedy on the true state, update~\eqref{eq:vanilla-q}, test $a=\arg\max_a Q(\tilde s,a)$ (or $Q(s,a)$ if clean).
Deep: Double DQN with Huber loss, MLP $Q_\theta$, greedy test on the (normalized) observation.

\subsection{Belief-enriched pessimistic Q (tabular)}
We follow Algorithms~1--2 of~\citet{sun2024belief} with two implementation notes that we state explicitly.
(i)~The discrete ball $B_\varepsilon(s)$ is the cell $s$ plus its on-grid $1$-step neighbors (up to $5$ states).
(ii)~When $|M\cap B_\varepsilon(\tilde s)|>1$, our tabular \emph{policy} uses the \emph{mean} Q over the belief rather than the hard $\min$ (a softer tabular approximation); the TD target still uses the maximin construction of~\citet{sun2024belief}.
Training: clone a vanilla Q-table, then $500$ fine-tune episodes with $\omega_\pi$ (or a learned DQN attacker at test).
Test under attack: update $M_t$ and act with the belief-restricted policy.
Clean test: greedy on the true state, for parity with vanilla/PB-GT clean columns.

\subsection{PB-GT Q-learning}
\label{sec:pbgt}
PB-GT keeps the \emph{data collection} of pessimistic Q-learning and changes only the backup and the test rule.

\paragraph{Training (tabular).}
At each step, form $\tilde s=\omega_\pi(s)$ (heuristic~\eqref{eq:omega} during learning), take an $\varepsilon$-greedy maximin action w.r.t.\ the current belief, update $M_t$ as in~\eqref{eq:belief}, but update Q with the \emph{vanilla} target:
\begin{equation}
Q(s,a)\leftarrow (1-\alpha)Q(s,a)+\alpha\bigl(R+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a')\bigr).
\label{eq:pbgt-tab}
\end{equation}
Belief is used to choose $a$, not to form the backup.

\paragraph{Testing.}
Ignore belief and maximin:
\begin{equation}
a=\arg\max_{a} Q(\tilde s,a).
\label{eq:pbgt-test}
\end{equation}
Inference cost therefore matches vanilla.

\paragraph{Why this might help.}
Pessimistic rollouts expose the Q-function to adversarial observations and to maximin actions, so the learned $Q$ can be more robust than vanilla even if the \emph{test} policy is greedy.
The cost of maximin is paid only during training.
The risk---confirmed on GridWorld---is that a greedy test policy can still be exploited by a strong attacker, so PB-GT should not be expected to match full pessimism.

\paragraph{Deep PB-GT DQN.}
Fine-tune from a pretrained vanilla DQN (optionally freeze the MLP backbone; train the last linear layer).
Collection matches BP-style training: random-search PGD in $B_\varepsilon(s)$ to form $\tilde s$, $\varepsilon$-greedy maximin over GRU particles $M_t$, store $(s,a,r,s',M')$ .
The loss uses Double-DQN greedy targets on the \emph{true} $s'$, \emph{not}
$y=r+\gamma\max_{a'}\min_{m\in M'}Q_{\theta'}(m,a')$.
Test is Algorithm~B: greedy on $\tilde s$.
Pseudocode is given in Appendix~\ref{app:alg}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PB-GT Q-learning (tabular)}
\label{alg:pbgt-tab}
\KwIn{pretrained $Q$; budget $\varepsilon$; episodes $E$}
\KwOut{fine-tuned $Q$}
$Q\leftarrow$ copy of pretrained table\;
\For{$e=1$ \KwTo $E$}{
  reset $s$; $M\leftarrow\mathcal{S}$\;
  \While{$s$ not terminal}{
    update $\pi$ from $Q$ with belief $M$\;
    $\tilde s\leftarrow\omega_\pi(s)$\;
    $a\leftarrow\varepsilon$-greedy maximin w.r.t.\ $M\cap B_\varepsilon(\tilde s)$\;
    execute $a$, observe $R,s'$\;
    $Q(s,a)\leftarrow (1-\alpha)Q(s,a)+\alpha(R+\gamma\max_{a'}Q(s',a'))$\;
    update $M$ by~\eqref{eq:belief}\;
    $s\leftarrow s'$\;
  }
}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Tabular GridWorld}
A custom $5{\times}5$ grid~\citep[related to the discrete setting discussed by][]{sun2024belief}.
Default cell reward $-1$; gold at $(0,3)$ with $+10$ (terminal); bomb at $(4,3)$ with $-10$ (terminal); four deterministic move actions with stay-on-wall.
$\gamma=1$, $\alpha$ linearly annealed, vanilla pretrain $2000$ episodes, robust fine-tune $500$ episodes from the \emph{same} Q initialization, evaluation $100$ episodes, episode cap $1000$ steps.
The discrete $\varepsilon$-ball is the $1$-step neighborhood.
We report mean episodic return $\pm$ standard deviation across \textbf{$500$ independent seeds}.

\paragraph{Attackers.}
We train Stable-Baselines3 DQN attackers once (frozen) against pessimistic and PB-GT defenders; attacker action is a perturbation direction in $\{\mathrm{UP},\mathrm{DOWN},\mathrm{LEFT},\mathrm{RIGHT},\mathrm{STAY}\}$.
At evaluation, vanilla is tested against the pessimistic attacker; pessimistic and PB-GT are tested against their own attackers (matched threat).
This is the protocol behind Table~\ref{tab:gw} and is \emph{not} identical to MinBest / PA-AD / iterative PGD in~\citet{sun2024belief}.

\subsection{CartPole-v1 (preliminary)}
Gymnasium CartPole-v1, observations linearly scaled to $[-1,1]$, attack budget $\varepsilon=0.05$ in raw coordinates ($L_\infty$).
Vanilla: Double DQN, MLP $[128,128]$, $5{\times}10^5$ steps, mid-eval + best-checkpoint restore (solve gate $\geq 475$).
Belief: GRU (hidden $64$) emitting $\kappa{=}20$ particles, pretrained $30$k steps on clean rollouts with synthetic noise---a simplified analogue of PF-RNN, \emph{not} $\kappa_p{=}30$ PF-LSTM BP-DQN.
Pessimistic fine-tune: clone vanilla, freeze backbone, LR $5{\cdot}10^{-5}$, up to $10^5$ steps, maximin TD target, early-stop / restore-best.
PB-GT fine-tune: same collection, greedy targets, $4{\times}10^4$ steps, LR $10^{-5}$.
\textbf{$n{=}5$ seeds} ($0$--$4$).
Hardware: Apple M1 Pro, CPU, PyTorch $2.8$, Gymnasium $1.1.1$.
\citet{sun2024belief} used an RTX $3090$; we flag this gap.

\paragraph{CartPole metrics} (mean return over $100$ eval episodes per seed).
(1)~Clean: greedy on true $s$ (pessimistic also logs maximin-on-clean separately).
(2)~Matched attacker: SB3 DQN, $81{=}3^4$ discrete $\delta$ per dimension, $80$k steps per (seed, defender); vanilla/PB-GT defend greedily, pessimistic with maximin-over-belief.
(3)~Shared random $\delta\sim\mathrm{Unif}([-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^4)$ from a shared RNG stream.
(4)~PGD*: $32$-candidate random search in $B_\varepsilon(s)$ scoring $Q(s,\pi(\tilde s))$---defender-specific, \emph{not} the $10$-step PGD ($\eta{=}10$) of~\citet{sun2024belief}.

Table~\ref{tab:gw} and Figures~\ref{fig:gw-bars}--\ref{fig:gw-box} are the main empirical result of this v1.
Clean returns are essentially identical (${\approx}7.35$): all three methods solve the short grid task when observations are intact.
Under attack, \textbf{pessimistic Q-learning dominates}: $-21.8{\pm}50.0$ versus $-552.4{\pm}335.2$ (PB-GT) and $-682.1{\pm}329.0$ (vanilla).
Pessimistic beats PB-GT in $96.4\%$ of seeds and vanilla in $96.0\%$; PB-GT beats vanilla in $60.6\%$ of seeds.
PB-GT is therefore a \emph{modest} improvement over vanilla on average, not a near-match to belief-maximin defense.

The compute side of the tradeoff is unambiguous.
Attack-time inference is $0.093$\,ms/step for pessimistic vs.\ $0.008$\,ms/step for PB-GT and vanilla---a \textbf{$11.6{\times}$} slowdown for full maximin (Figure~\ref{fig:speed}).
This matches the motivation in~\citet{sun2024belief} that robustness from belief search is not free, and it is the reason PB-GT exists.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These numbers support two claims and refute a third.
(1)~Belief-enriched maximin, even in this small discrete grid with a mean-over-belief policy approximation, is highly effective against a learned DQN attacker---consistent with the tabular motivation of~\citet{sun2024belief}.
(2)~Pessimistic \emph{behavior} alone (PB-GT) is not enough to capture most of that robustness if the test policy is greedy on $\tilde s$.
(3)~We do \emph{not} claim that PB-GT ``approaches BP-DQN robustness.''
The gap of ${\approx}530$ return between pessimistic and PB-GT is the open problem for later versions of this line of work (hybrid inference, distillation, mixed targets; see \S\ref{sec:limit}).

We keep CartPole as evidence that the \emph{pipeline} runs end-to-end and that the inference gap persists, and we move the main deep comparison to future continuous-GridWorld work.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{State-observation attacks} on RL were demonstrated by~\citet{huang2017adversarial} and developed into SA-MDP~\citep{zhang2020robust}, ATLA~\citep{zhang2021robust}, and WocaR~\citep{liang2022wocar}; see~\citet{ilahi2022challenges} for a broader survey.
\textbf{Pessimism and beliefs.}
\citet{sun2024belief} is the direct predecessor of this paper: Stackelberg formulation, maximin Q, tabular belief, BP-DQN, DP-DQN.
We reuse their tabular algorithms and study a cheaper test-time rule.
\textbf{Other robust DQN.}
CAR-DQN~\citep{li2024car} minimizes a surrogate of Bellman $\infty$-error under a consistency assumption; we list it as a future baseline, not a result we have.
PF-RNN~\citep{ma2020pfrnn} is the belief module in BP-DQN; our CartPole GRU is a simplified stand-in.
We do not claim novelty over SA-DQN / WocaR / ATLA / CAR-DQN on Atari---those comparisons require the continuous GridWorld / Atari bake-off outlined in \S\ref{sec:limit}.

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limit}

\paragraph{What this v1 does \emph{not} show.}
PB-GT does not approach pessimistic / BP-DQN robustness on GridWorld.
CartPole is preliminary and currently negative for both robust methods.
We have not reproduced Table~1a of~\citet{sun2024belief} (continuous GridWorld, BP-DQN, PGD / MinBest / PA-AD).
We have not run CAR-DQN~\citep{li2024car}, SA-DQN, WocaR, or Atari Pong/Freeway.
PGD* on CartPole is a $32$-sample random search, not iterative PGD.
Vanilla GridWorld is evaluated against the pessimistic attacker, not a vanilla-matched attacker.

\paragraph{Natural next experiments} (in the spirit of~\citet{sun2024belief}, \S4).
(1)~\textbf{Continuous GridWorld + official BP-DQN}, then add PB-GT under the same eval protocol.
(2)~\textbf{CAR-DQN} as an additional robust DQN baseline.
(3)~\textbf{PB-GT refinements} aimed at Zheng's criterion---approach BP-DQN robustness at much lower overhead: hybrid greedy/maximin inference, softmin targets, policy distillation from a pessimistic teacher, mixed maximin/greedy backups.
(4)~Atari (Pong/Freeway) + DP-DQN on GPU, after (1)--(3).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}
We compared vanilla Q-learning, belief-enriched pessimistic Q-learning~\citep{sun2024belief}, and PB-GT---pessimistic behavior, greedy target, greedy test---on a $500$-seed tabular GridWorld and a $5$-seed CartPole Double-DQN port.
The discrete result is clear: full pessimism is far more robust under learned attacks; PB-GT is far cheaper at test time and only slightly better than vanilla.
The deep port is not yet a robustness result.
A scoped reading of this v1 is therefore: \emph{the robustness--compute tradeoff of belief-maximin Q-learning is real; PB-GT is a well-defined fast baseline that does not close the gap}.
Closing that gap, on the same environments used by~\citet{sun2024belief}, is the natural next paper.

\section{PB-GT DQN pseudocode}
\label{app:alg}
Format mirrors Appendix~E, Algorithm~4 of~\citet{sun2024belief}. Differences from BP-DQN are in \textbf{bold}.

\paragraph{Algorithm A: PB-GT DQN --- training.}
\textbf{Input:} iterations $T$; pretrained vanilla $Q_v$; belief model $N_p$; target-update period $Z$; batch $D$; exploration $\varepsilon'$; budget $\varepsilon$; PGD candidates $K$.\\
\textbf{Output:} fine-tuned $Q_g$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item $\mathcal{B}\leftarrow\emptyset$; $Q_g\leftarrow Q_v$; $Q'\leftarrow Q_v$; init $N_p$ history.
\item \textbf{Optionally freeze all layers of $Q_g$ except the last linear head; use a small LR.}
\item For $t=0,\ldots,T{-}1$:
\item\quad Random-search PGD ($K$ candidates) for $\tilde s_t\in B_\varepsilon(s_t)$ minimizing $Q_g(s_t,\pi(\tilde s_t))$.
\item\quad Form $M_t$ from $N_p$ (or sample in $B_\varepsilon(\tilde s_t)$).
\item\quad $a_t=\arg\max_a\min_{m\in M_t}Q_g(m,a)$ w.p.\ $1-\varepsilon'$, else random.
\item\quad Update $N_p$ history; execute $a_t$; observe $R_t$ and \textbf{true} $s_{t+1}$.
\item\quad Store $(s_t,a_t,R_t,s_{t+1},M_{t+1})$ in $\mathcal{B}$ (belief stored for parity; \textbf{unused in the target}).
\item\quad Sample minibatch of size $D$. Set \textbf{greedy Double-DQN targets}
\[
y_i=
\begin{cases}
R_i & \text{if }s_{i+1}\text{ terminal,}\\
R_i+\gamma\,Q'\bigl(s_{i+1},\arg\max_{a'}Q_g(s_{i+1},a')\bigr) & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
\textbf{Do not} use $\max_{a'}\min_{m\in M_{i+1}}Q'(m,a')$.
\item\quad Gradient step on Huber $\sum_i (y_i-Q_g(s_i,a_i))^2$; update $Q'$ every $Z$ episodes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Algorithm B: PB-GT DQN --- testing.}
For each step: observe $\tilde s_t$; take $a_t=\arg\max_a Q_g(\tilde s_t,a)$ (\textbf{no} $M_t$, \textbf{no} maximin); execute $a_t$.

\section{Hyperparameters}
\label{app:hyper}

\paragraph{GridWorld.}
Size $5{\times}5$; $R{=}-1$ default, $+10$ gold, $-10$ bomb; $\gamma{=}1$; $\alpha$ annealed $0.1\to 0.005$ (vanilla) / $0.1\to 0.01$ (robust); $\varepsilon$-greedy $0.2\to 0.01$ during robust train; vanilla $2000$ episodes; pes./PB-GT $500$ fine-tune episodes; eval $100$ episodes; max steps $1000$; attacker SB3 DQN, $\mathrm{MlpPolicy}$, $[256,128]$, $2{\times}10^5$ steps, lr $10^{-3}$, $\gamma{=}1$.

\paragraph{CartPole vanilla DQN.}
MLP $[128,128]$ ReLU; Adam $10^{-3}$; $\gamma{=}0.99$; buffer $10^5$; batch $128$; learn start $10^3$; train every $4$ steps; target copy every $10$ episodes; $\varepsilon$: $1.0\to 0.01$ over $50$k steps; Huber; grad clip $10$; $5{\times}10^5$ steps (+ continue if clean ${<}475$).

\paragraph{Belief (CartPole).}
GRU hidden $64$; $\kappa{=}20$; $30$k pretrain steps; Adam $10^{-3}$; mode \texttt{learned}.

\paragraph{Pessimistic fine-tune.}
Clone vanilla; freeze backbone; LR $5{\cdot}10^{-5}$; $\leq 10^5$ steps; $\varepsilon$ $0.02\to 0.005$ / $15$k; mid-eval every $25$k; early stop after best greedy $\geq 475$ if greedy ${<}400$ or drop ${>}75$; restore best; maximin target and test.

\paragraph{PB-GT fine-tune.}
Clone vanilla; freeze backbone; LR $10^{-5}$; $4{\times}10^4$ steps; $\varepsilon$ $0.01\to 0.005$ / $10$k; pessimistic behavior + PGD $\tilde s$; greedy target; greedy test.

\paragraph{Matched attackers (CartPole).}
SB3 DQN $[128,128]$; $8{\times}10^4$ steps per (seed, defender); lr $10^{-3}$; buffer $5{\times}10^4$; batch $128$; $\gamma{=}0.99$; explore fraction $0.3$, final $\varepsilon{=}0.05$.
